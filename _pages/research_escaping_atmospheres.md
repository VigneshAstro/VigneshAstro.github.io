<p class="research-topic-intro">
  Metastable helium at 1083 nm is a powerful tracer of gas escaping from irradiated planets. My work uses high- and low-resolution
  transmission spectroscopy and time-resolved observations to measure thermospheres, outflows, and extended leading and trailing tails.
  Taken together, detections and carefully measured non-detections reveal which planets retain primordial envelopes, how irradiation shapes
  escaping gas, and how atmospheric loss helps sculpt the observed planet population.
</p>

    <div class="research-feature__copy">
      <time datetime="2025-12">December 2025</time>
      <h3><a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-025-02710-8">Continuous helium absorption from both tails of WASP-107 b</a></h3>
      <p>
        Using a continuous JWST/NIRISS observation, we detected helium absorption beginning well before the optical transit and persisting
        after it. The signal traces both a leading structure and a much longer trailing tail, revealing an escaping thermosphere extending
        tens of planetary radii. Three-dimensional EvE simulations reproduce the asymmetric helium light curve and show how the stellar wind,
        radiation pressure, and orbital motion shape the outflow.
      </p>
      <p>
        This result turns the transit into a time-resolved map of atmospheric escape: instead of measuring only the gas directly in front of
        the planet, we follow material distributed along its orbit.
      </p>

      <h3><a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-66628-5">Escaping helium around WASP-121 b</a></h3>
      <p>
        In this Nature Communications study led by Romain Allart, a continuous JWST/NIRISS phase curve revealed helium absorption over nearly
        60% of the orbit. The gas separates into leading and trailing structures that span more than half the orbit, demonstrating that escape
        from highly irradiated planets can form complex, planet-scale and orbit-scale morphologies.
      </p>

      <time datetime="2025-08">August 2025</time>
      <h3><a href="https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202452525">NIRPS and the delayed helium escape of WASP-69 b</a></h3>
      <p>
        As part of the NIRPS collaboration, I contributed to a three-transit study that recovered a 3.17% helium signal and a delayed
        post-transit tail. EvE simulations connect the time-variable line profile to the three-dimensional outflow, while the Rossiter-McLaughlin
        measurement places the escape in the context of the planet's mildly misaligned orbit. This work builds on the instrument's
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202453341">on-sky performance and science programme</a> and
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3018994">early-science results</a>.
      </p>

      <time datetime="2024-07">July 2024</time>
      <h3><a href="https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ad5441">Where is helium most readily detected?</a></h3>
      <p>
        Krishnamurthy &amp; Cowan re-analysed 17 detections and 40 non-detections to identify the stellar and orbital conditions that favour
        metastable-helium absorption. The sample reveals an orbital "sweet spot" at roughly 0.03-0.08 au, dependencies on the stellar XUV-to-MUV
        radiation balance, and population-level trends that help distinguish physical absence from observational selection.
      </p>
      <div class="research-link-row">

  <article class="research-feature research-feature--compact">
    <div class="research-feature__copy">
      <time datetime="2023-04">April 2023</time>
      <h3><a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stad404">No extended helium around three radius-gap planets</a></h3>
      <p>
        Subaru/IRD transit spectra of GJ 9827 b, GJ 9827 d, and TOI-1235 b yielded stringent helium upper limits. Coupled with wind calculations
        and the planets' bulk properties, these non-detections are more consistent with photoevaporation and/or core-powered mass loss than
        with gas-poor formation alone. Non-detections are therefore physical constraints, not empty results: they test whether a low-density
        envelope is still present and escaping.
      </p>
      <div class="research-link-row">
        <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.521.1210K/abstract">NASA ADS</a>

    <div class="research-feature__copy">
      <time datetime="2021-08">August 2021</time>
      <h3><a href="https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ac0d91">Helium non-detections for TRAPPIST-1 b, e, and f</a></h3>
      <p>
        High-resolution IRD and HPF observations found no evidence for extended, primordial hydrogen-helium atmospheres around these three
        terrestrial planets. The upper limits narrow the permitted mass-loss regimes and shift attention toward higher-mean-molecular-weight
        atmospheres - an important step in understanding what kinds of atmospheres the TRAPPIST-1 planets may retain.
      </p>
      <div class="research-link-row">
        <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021AJ....162...82K/abstract">NASA ADS</a>
