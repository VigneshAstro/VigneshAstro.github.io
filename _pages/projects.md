I use spectroscopy and precision transit observations to investigate the composition, structure, and evolution of exoplanet atmospheres. A recurring goal is to connect atmospheric measurements to the formation histories and present-day environments of planets.

## Escaping atmospheres and extended helium structures

Metastable helium at 1083 nm is a powerful tracer of gas escaping from irradiated planets. I study how helium absorption changes before, during, and after transit, revealing thermospheres and tails that can extend tens of planetary radii. Our JWST observations of WASP-107 b detected continuous absorption across the transit, including prominent leading and trailing structures.

## The radius gap around low-mass stars

The scarcity of planets at particular radii offers a direct test of atmospheric evolution. Using high-resolution near-infrared transit spectroscopy, I search for escaping helium around super-Earths and sub-Neptunes near the radius gap. Constraints for GJ 9827 b and d and TOI-1235 b help distinguish atmospheric-loss scenarios from gas-poor formation.

## Young planets and primordial atmospheres

Young planets preserve evidence of their initial atmospheric conditions while orbiting magnetically active stars. I observe systems such as AU Mic and DS Tuc to constrain atmospheric escape, stellar contamination, and spin-orbit geometry during the first stages of planetary evolution.

## Compact systems and transit-timing variations

Closely packed multi-planet systems produce measurable departures from strictly periodic transits. I combine observations from facilities such as Mont-Mégantic Observatory with archival TESS and K2 light curves to measure transit-timing variations and infer planetary masses and orbital architectures.

## Temperate terrestrial planets

Nearby M dwarfs provide some of the best opportunities to study small planets. My work on TRAPPIST-1 constrains primordial hydrogen-helium atmospheres, while collaborative discoveries such as Gliese 12 b identify temperate targets suited to future atmospheric observations.
